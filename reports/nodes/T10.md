# T10 Node Report: Research and Method Landscape

## Objective

Map related work and methodological precedents for historically time-consistent language models, historical knowledge boundaries, anachronism evaluation, historical RAG, and lightweight adaptation.

## Dependencies

T00.

## Questions Resolved

- Temporal NLP work provides strong precedents for time-aware evaluation and train/test time reporting, but it is mostly based on modern data.
- Historical language-model work exists for English and broad Classical Chinese, but not for late-Qing public-health genres as a focused domain.
- RAG and attribution work are relevant because this project needs source-grounded answers and calibrated uncertainty.
- DAPT, LoRA, and QLoRA are feasible technical routes, but they do not themselves prevent anachronism or enforce historically appropriate ignorance.
- Late-Qing public-health scholarship, especially work on `weisheng` and plague, points to conceptual drift and causal/value-anachronism risks.

## Actual Outcome

The literature matrix now covers five families:

1. Temporal mismatch and time-aware factual probes.
2. Historical-domain and Classical Chinese NLP.
3. Diachronic semantics.
4. Retrieval-augmented generation and attribution.
5. Lightweight adaptation and late-Qing public-health history.

## Key Findings

- Verified methodological precedent exists for temporal evaluation, historical-domain adaptation, RAG, and lightweight adaptation.
- The field does not appear to offer an off-the-shelf, source-grounded, pre-1900 Chinese public-health anachronism benchmark.
- The most valuable research gap is therefore evaluative and evidentiary first: define what a model may responsibly claim given date, source, provenance, and accessible historical communities.

## Changed Assumptions

- Large model adaptation should remain a later probe; benchmark and corpus feasibility are higher-value first-round work.
- Post-1900 materials can be useful as contrastive anachronism foils, but should not be treated as evidence for the pre-1900 cutoff.

## Risks

- Several history sources are secondary monographs/articles and require close reading before supporting detailed historical claims.
- Modern temporal NLP benchmarks may be misleading if imported directly into sparse archival settings.

## Follow-up Tasks

- T20 should generate candidates centered on source-grounded evaluation, not only model tuning.
- T40 should separate factual, lexical, causal, value, and attribution anachronism.
