# T11 Node Report: Historical Source and Access Landscape

## Objective

Map archives, databases, licensing, access, OCR, metadata, and bulk-processing feasibility for late-Qing public-health materials.

## Dependencies

T00.

## Questions Resolved

- Relevant source families exist for late-Qing public health, but they differ sharply in openness, corpus scale, language, and bias.
- Open small-probe candidates are strongest in English-language treaty-port, customs, missionary, and municipal collections.
- High-value Chinese newspapers and periodical databases exist, but many are subscription platforms and poor candidates for unauthorised bulk processing.
- Gazetteers and central archives are important for governance context but may be harder to search and less suitable for a first OCR/RAG pilot.

## Inputs

- T11 subagent source map.
- Official archive/library/catalogue pages listed in `evidence/source_registry.md`.
- Project access and ethics constraints.

## Planned Actions

- Build a source registry with access, terms, OCR, metadata, bias, and probe status fields.
- Identify candidate source families for T30/T31.
- Record blocked directions rather than bypassing access constraints.

## Actual Outcome

The source registry now records twelve source families:

- Chinese Maritime Customs databases and digitized customs collections.
- China Medical Missionary Journal and missionary periodicals.
- Shen Bao and late-Qing periodical databases.
- Dianshizhai Pictorial.
- Local gazetteers and Qing central archives.
- Shanghai Municipal Archives and Shanghai Municipal Council annual reports.
- North-China Herald.

## Key Findings

- The most immediately probeable materials are `SRC-CUSTOMS-WELLCOME`, `SRC-CMMJ-CRL`, `SRC-CUSTOMS-AS`, and `SRC-SMC-BNASIE`, because they appear to provide open item-level access or web interfaces.
- The strongest Chinese-language corpora, especially `SRC-SHENBAO` and `SRC-LQPERIODICALS`, are valuable but access-limited; they should not be treated as bulk data sources without institutional authorization.
- Source population is a major design constraint. The accessible pilot corpus would overrepresent treaty ports, foreign-run institutions, missionaries, urban elites, and English-language or translated medical categories.

## Changed Assumptions

- The first pilot should probably frame itself as a treaty-port/missionary/customs source-ecology benchmark, not as a representation of all late-Qing public-health knowledge.
- Chinese full-text periodical/newspaper corpora remain viable only as authorized-access extensions or manually sampled references.

## Risks and Blockers

- Subscription databases and no-batch-download rules block unauthorised bulk use of several high-value corpora.
- OCR may be poor for vertical traditional Chinese, variant characters, mixed Chinese-English layouts, tables, advertisements, and pictorial captions.
- Metadata granularity differs across source families, making a uniform benchmark schema necessary.

## Follow-up Tasks

- T30 should attempt small, lawful access probes for `SRC-CUSTOMS-WELLCOME`, `SRC-CMMJ-CRL`, and `SRC-SMC-BNASIE`.
- T31 should test OCR/text quality only on open or permission-compatible samples.
- Candidate topics should explicitly state source population and bias.
